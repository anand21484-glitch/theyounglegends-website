Learning together

Six ways to make every session count.

Small habits that turn ten minutes on a screen into a conversation that lasts all week.

  1. Ask before you answer

    When your child asks “why?”, try “what do you think?” first.

  2. Predict together

    Before an experiment, both of you make a guess — and see who was closer.

  3. Retell the story

    At dinner, ask them to tell you the hero’s story in their own words.

  4. Celebrate wrong guesses

    A wrong prediction is how scientists learn. Make it a good moment.

  5. Connect it to the world

    Spot the science or history around you: in the kitchen, on a walk, in the news.

  6. Keep sessions short

    Ten curious minutes beat an hour of tapping. Stop while they still want more.

Questions from parents

Practical answers.

What ages are the apps designed for?

Roughly 5 to 15. Younger children enjoy them most alongside a parent; older children can explore independently.

Is the Curiosity Score a test of intelligence?

No. It celebrates how a child explores — asking, revisiting, trying again — and is never a ranking of ability.

Do the apps work offline?

Azadi Tales supports offline learning. Offline support for other apps is listed on each app’s page.

Is Azadi Tales political?

No. It tells the stories of the people who fought for India’s freedom, focused on courage, values and understanding history.

Are the experiments safe to do at home?

They use everyday household materials and are designed to be done with an adult nearby, especially for younger children.

How do I contact you about my child’s data?

Use the contact page and choose “Parent question”. Our Privacy Policy explains what we collect and why.
