Why Azadi Tales

History shouldn’t feel far away.

For most children, the freedom struggle is a list of dates to memorise. But it was made by people — many of them young — making brave choices. That is what children remember.

In a textbook

Dates, names and paragraphs.

  • Events without the people behind them
  • Memorised for a test, forgotten after
  • No room to ask “why did they do that?”

In Azadi Tales

Real heroes, real choices.

  • Illustrated stories told from the hero’s point of view
  • A timeline children can walk through, moment by moment
  • Quizzes that ask what they would have done

Story first

Every lesson begins as a story with a person, a problem and a choice.

Values, not slogans

Courage, kindness and leadership shown through what people actually did.

Learning that sticks

Quizzes, puzzles and badges bring children back to what they read.

Meet the freedom fighters

The people behind India’s freedom.

1869 – 1948

Mahatma Gandhi

Showed the world that truth and non-violence could move an empire.

1828 – 1858

Rani Lakshmibai

The queen of Jhansi who rode into battle in 1857 to defend her people.

1907 – 1931

Bhagat Singh

A young reader and writer whose courage inspired a generation.

1879 – 1949

Sarojini Naidu

Poet and leader, known as the Nightingale of India.

1897 – 1945

Subhas Chandra Bose

Netaji, who led the Indian National Army in the fight for freedom.

1906 – 1931

Chandrashekhar Azad

Took the name “Azad” — free — and lived up to it.

1875 – 1950

Sardar Patel

The Iron Man of India, who helped unite hundreds of princely states.

And many more heroes inside the app.

Is Azadi Tales political?

No. It tells the stories of the people who fought for India’s freedom, with a focus on courage, values and understanding history — not on present-day politics.

How long is each story?

Most stories take five to ten minutes, which makes them easy to fit into a day.

Does it need an internet connection?

Azadi Tales supports offline learning, so stories can be enjoyed anywhere.
