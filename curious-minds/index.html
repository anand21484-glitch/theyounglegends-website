Scientist connections

Physics · Nobel Prize 1930

Asked why the sea is blue — and discovered how light scatters.

Astronomy · 5th century

Aryabhata

Explained that the Earth spins on its axis, over 1,500 years ago.

Physics & chemistry · Two Nobel Prizes

Marie Curie

Discovered radium and polonium, and changed medicine forever.

Aerospace

A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

From a boy selling newspapers to the Missile Man of India.

Agricultural science

M. S. Swaminathan

Helped India grow enough food to feed itself.

Physics & mathematics

Isaac Newton

Wondered why things fall, and described gravity.

For parents & educators

Building the habit of asking why.

Scientific temperament

Children practise predicting, testing and changing their minds when the evidence says so.

Safe, simple materials

Experiments use everyday household items, designed to be done with a grown-up nearby.

Curiosity Score, not IQ

It celebrates asking, exploring and trying again — never a ranking of ability.

Classroom-ready

Experiments work just as well in a classroom as at home.

Do I need special equipment?

No. Experiments are built around things most homes already have: water, salt, paper, seeds, cups and spoons.

Are the experiments safe?

They use everyday, household materials and are designed to be done with an adult nearby, especially for younger children.

Can schools use Curious Minds?

Yes — get in touch through our educator pathway to talk about classroom use.
